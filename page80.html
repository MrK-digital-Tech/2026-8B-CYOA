Throw up and end up in hospital

Patricia, a Japanese teenage girl with long black hair and warm brown eyes, stood in the middle of the bustling city street, her red long-sleeve shirt contrasting with her beige cargo pants and white Nike shoes. The noise of traffic and chatter faded as a sudden, violent nausea overtook her. She stumbled to the side of the sidewalk, her hands pressing against her stomach, but it was too late—she vomited right there on the pavement, her long hair swinging forward as she retched, her white Nikes splattered.
Passersby averted their eyes or hurried past, but no one stopped. Patricia, trembling and embarrassed, wiped her mouth with the sleeve of her red shirt, her face burning with humiliation. She tried to stand straight, but another wave hit her, and she sank to her knees, her beige cargo pants dusty from the city grime. A stranger finally called an ambulance, and as the paramedics arrived, Patricia could only focus on the spinning lights and the cold sweat on her skin, her once-clean white Nikes now a messy testament to the worst day of her life.
