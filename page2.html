At school gates

The morning light rested over the school gate with an almost unnatural clarity, a quiet brightness that revealed everything yet comforted nothing. Patricia stood still in that sharpened calm, holding the leather notebook that felt both ancient and newly awakened. Its surface was smooth as river stone, as if countless hands had carried it through forgotten histories. The air around her hummed with the soft chaos of students drifting past, a kind of organised confusion that made her solitude feel strangely loud, a perfect oxymoron that mirrored the tension in her chest.

She opened the notebook by a fraction. The symbols inside did not simply sit on the page. They watched. They breathed. They resisted interpretation with the stubborn dignity of secrets that had survived too long to surrender easily. They were a silent storm, a still movement, a contradiction that demanded attention. A shadow crossed the page. Her friends steped foward with the careless curiosity of someone who did not yet understand the weight of what they were touching. In a single swift motion, the notebook was taken from her hands. The gate buzzed open behind them, a mechanical heartbeat announcing the start of an ordinary day that had already become extraordinary. Patricia felt the moment shift with the quiet force of a whispered verdict. Something had begun, and it would not wait.
