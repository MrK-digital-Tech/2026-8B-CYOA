At The Airport

Patricia sat in a distant corner of Charles de Gaulle Airport, a place where the fluorescent lights glowed with a cold warmth, an oxymoron that suited the sterile comfort of the space. The airport breathed around her, a living machine of rolling suitcases, murmured announcements, and travellers drifting like restless tides. She felt both invisible and exposed, a paradox that clung to her after escaping the police station. She unfolded the brochure she had taken from the arrivals desk. The image on the cover showed a Canadian ski resort resting beneath a blanket of impossibly pure snow. It looked peaceful yet dangerous, serene yet severe, a landscape built entirely from contradictions. The chairlift rising into the fog resembled a question suspended in the sky, waiting for an answer she was not sure she could give.

As she read, the words seemed to shift, not literally but in meaning, as if the brochure itself understood urgency. Her uncle had always said that some discoveries refused to wait, that knowledge could be both a burden and a beacon. She felt that truth now, pressing against her like a silent alarm. When she closed the brochure, she did so with the deliberate calm of someone stepping across an unseen threshold. It was not a decision. Not yet. But it was the first moment since the police station that did not demand her defence. And in that fragile certainty, she found the strength to continue.
