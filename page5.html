Police are calling your parents.

Patricia sat in the cramped interview room, the fluorescent lights buzzing overhead as an officer dialed her parents’ number. Her heart thudded like it was trying to punch its way out of her chest. She knew the moment her parents arrived, everything would spiral. Then she noticed it the officer had stepped out, leaving the door slightly ajar. Beyond it, she could see the station’s lobby and, past that, the front door leading straight onto the busy street. It was risky, exposed, and every instinct told her she’d be spotted instantly.
But there was another option. A narrow hallway branched off to the right, dim and quiet, ending in the back door used mostly by staff. It might be locked. It might have alarms. It might lead straight into a fenced off yard. Still, it was a chance maybe her only one. Patricia inhaled sharply, adrenaline sharpening her thoughts. Two doors. Two futures. One decision she had to make right now.

Patricia bursts through the front doors of the police station, sunlight hitting her like a slap. Her breath is already ragged, her pulse hammering in her ears. Behind her, the shouts start—sharp, commanding, too close. Boots hit the concrete as officers spill out after her, the radio chatter crackling with urgency. She doesn’t look back. She just runs, legs burning, weaving between parked cars as sirens begin to wail somewhere deeper in the city.
