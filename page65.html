Uncle Finds the Map

Patricia arrived at her uncle’s small apartment, the air thick with the scent of old paper and dust. Maps covered the walls—some faded, some freshly inked—and in the center of the cluttered desk lay the one she’d come for: a hand‑drawn chart of the ancient ruins in Myanmar. Her uncle’s eyes gleamed with excitement as he traced the route with his finger, explaining how the markings led to a forgotten temple buried deep in the jungle. It was the culmination of years of research, and Patricia could feel the pulse of adventure in her veins. But before they could plan their journey, the sound of heavy footsteps echoed down the hall—a warning that their secret had been discovered.

Within hours, everything unraveled. Officials stormed in, accusing her uncle of trespassing on restricted archaeological grounds. Patricia watched helplessly as he was taken away, his voice fading behind the clatter of handcuffs and shouted orders. The next morning, she learned he had been deported, leaving the map behind as the only trace of his work. Alone in the quiet apartment, Patricia unfolded the parchment once more. The ink shimmered faintly under the light, and she realized that the choice now rested with her—to follow the path her uncle had uncovered or to let the ruins remain buried in history.
