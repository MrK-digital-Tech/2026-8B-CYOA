The Eiffel Tower Lookout

Patricia stepped off the plane with a mix of jet lag and quiet excitement humming in her veins. The crisp Parisian air carried the faint scent of fresh croissants and diesel as she navigated the bustling streets. She carefully opened the worn leather notebook her uncle had sent to her. Its pages were filled with his elegant handwriting and intricate sketches.

Tucked between notes on forgotten cafés and obscure museums was a detailed drawing of the Eiffel Tower’s second-level lookout platform, complete with tiny arrows pointing toward specific landmarks visible from that height. A single underlined phrase in the margin read: “Look to the horizon—see what I saw.”

Heart quickening, Patricia slipped the notebook into her bag and headed straight for the iconic iron lattice structure. Standing at the railing of the Eiffel Tower’s observation deck, the city unfolded beneath her like a living map. The spring sunlight glinted off the Seine as she turned slowly, scanning the horizon exactly as the sketch had indicated. There, to the southeast, rose the grand glass pyramid and classical wings of the Louvre, its vast courtyards bustling even from this distance. Slightly to the west, the majestic Arc de Triomphe stood proudly at the center of the étoile, its massive form anchoring the Champs-Élysées like a sentinel of history.

But which landmark did her uncle mean? Where should she go?
