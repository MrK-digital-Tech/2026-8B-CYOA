A teenage girl with long black hair and steady brown eyes stood in the small, fluorescent‑lit office at the back of the station. Her red long‑sleeve shirt looked too bright against the dull walls, and her beige cargo pants were still dusty from where she’d been sitting outside earlier. When the officer reached for the phone to call her parents, she felt her chest tighten. She didn’t wait to hear the first ring. The moment his attention shifted, she slipped quietly toward the back door, her white Nike shoes barely making a sound on the linoleum.

Outside, the cool air hit her face as she pushed the door open and stepped into the alley. Her heart pounded, but she kept moving, scanning the lot behind the building. Then she saw it—her parents’ car, parked crookedly as if they’d rushed inside. She jogged toward it, her hair swinging behind her, hoping she could reach it before anyone realized she was gone.
