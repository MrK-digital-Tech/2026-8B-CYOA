Saw Uncle talking with men about going to ski in Banff

Patricia crouched behind the shed, the dry grass brushing her knees as she watched her uncle speaking in low, urgent tones to two unfamiliar men. She heard the word Banff more than once — spoken like a secret, like a destination that meant far more than a simple trip. The men kept glancing around as if they were afraid of being seen, and Patricia felt a knot tighten in her stomach. Whatever they were planning, it wasn’t a holiday. She clutched her backpack strap, torn between stepping out and demanding answers or slipping away before anyone noticed she’d been listening.

As she backed up, her mind raced. She could follow them to Banff and uncover the truth herself, staying close enough to understand what her uncle had gotten tangled in. Or she could run to the police station, where she’d have to explain everything in shaky, uncertain French — the only language the officer on duty spoke today. The thought of stumbling through her words made her cheeks burn, but the idea of letting something dangerous unfold without warning anyone felt worse. Patricia stood at the crossroads of courage and fear, knowing that whichever path she chose would change everything.
