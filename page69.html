Flying Back But Something Caught Your Eye

Patricia pressed her forehead to the airplane window, watching the mountains roll beneath her like a frozen ocean. Then she saw it — the jagged peak from her uncle’s notebook, the one he’d circled and annotated with a shaky hand. At the base of the mountain, half-hidden by shadow, was the unmistakable dark mouth of a cave. Her pulse kicked up. This wasn’t just a coincidence. Her uncle had written about something hidden there, something he never had the chance to explain. Now it was right below her, real and waiting.

The captain’s voice crackled overhead, announcing their descent toward the airport, but Patricia barely heard it. She was caught between two choices: stay on the flight, land safely, and hand the notebook over to the authorities like she’d promised — or convince the pilot to make an emergency landing and investigate the cave herself. One path meant responsibility, rules, and safety. The other meant stepping straight into the mystery her uncle had left behind. The plane kept moving forward, but Patricia felt suspended between the sky and the mountain, between duty and discovery.
