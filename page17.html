By the time Patricia’s boots hit the ground, her hands were shaking. The climb down from Christ the Redeemer had felt longer than the climb up. Her palms were raw, her breathing uneven. The guards hadn’t noticed her — or maybe they chose not to. Either way , she was standing now, alone at the base of Rio’s most famous monument, clutching the note she had pulled from the statue’s hand. The paper was damp from the clouds that brushed the mountaintop , edges torn from the wind. She unfolded it again, even though she already knew what it said. “I'm being followed to France, it's not safe to trust anyone", her favourite uncles name signed at the bottom. She stood there for a while, simply staring at the ink that bled slightly into the fibers of the page.

It didn’t make sense. No one in Brazil knew her. She’d flown here from Australia three days ago — just another tourist with a backpack and a secret ache for adventure. But her uncle… The one who used to send her little gifts, letters that smelled faintly of espresso and rain. The one who disappeared last spring without warning. Patricia ran her thumb over the words again, her long black braid sticking to her neck in the humid air. The city stretched far below the mountain, glittering under the afternoon sun. “Airport or police,” she murmured, voice barely a whisper. The choice sat heavy in her chest. If this was a prank, it was too specific. Too cruel. If it wasn’t… She looked up once more at the massive statue above her — the open arms, the expression of calm carved into stone. It almost felt like the Redeemer had handed her the message himself. Patricia took a deep breath, folded the note, and tucked it into her jacket. Her heart was pounding again, but this time it wasn’t fear. It was resolve. She turned away from the statue and started walking...
